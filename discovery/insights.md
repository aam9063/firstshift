# Insights: crew hiring discovery, Costa Blanca pilot

## 60-second readout (for the CPO)

Lumbre does not have one hiring process, it has three wearing the same name, and the one HQ described is the slowest of them. The funnel data puts the biggest leak at the front door: 41% of 4,500 applications were never contacted, and the candidates we interviewed take the first chain that answers, within the hour, not the best job. But the real discovery headline is further downstream: between "she said yes" and "she is on the rota" sit document chasing, a 3-to-6-day employee creation in Nómina360 and a Thursday rota lock, and nobody owns that window. It is the largest time sink in our seeded funnel (5.1 days median, the biggest stage gap of all), it is where signed hires vanish to competitors three streets away, and no system, team or report is responsible for it. Meanwhile the actual hiring machine runs on the managers' personal WhatsApp: a bench of about 25 "maybes" per manager that is faster than every official channel and invisible to every system we would integrate with. The opportunity is not a faster ATS. It is owning the hired-to-rota window, answering every applicant fast on WhatsApp, and making the informal bench official instead of pretending it does not exist.

---

## The insights

> **Insight:** First-contact speed decides who wins the candidate. The winning move is not the better job, it is the first concrete reply, and the funnel's 3.9-day median reply time loses to competitors who answer in minutes.
> **Evidence:** [T-candidate-a-08 to T-candidate-a-12] (applied at 18:00 via QR, WhatsApp reply before 19:00, the only one of six chains that answered, and she took it); funnel output in `discovery/funnel-baseline.md` (median 3.9 days to first contact, 41.0% never contacted); [T-candidate-b-18] ("a quick no is better than a slow maybe"); [T-candidate-c-12] (one week waiting for a manager call, took the beach bar contract instead).
> **So what for design:** the agent's first message must go out on WhatsApp in minutes, name the restaurant, and ask one concrete question; every applicant gets an answer, including a fast no. Speed is the product, not a feature.

> **Insight:** Availability is collected as a vague dropdown and then treated as a dead field. The candidates with the most valuable availability get rejected on it without anyone ever asking which shifts they mean.
> **Evidence:** [T-candidate-b-04, T-candidate-b-08 to T-candidate-b-10] (rejected twice for "availability"; nobody asked; his real availability is evenings and all weekend, exactly the hours restaurants suffer, in his words [T-candidate-b-24]); [T-candidate-a-20] (answered "completa" to the dropdown because she did not understand it, though she can only work mornings); [T-churned-14] (availability stated in the interview never reached the rota, which scheduled her into a university morning).
> **So what for design:** collect availability as concrete shifts through conversation ("can you do Saturday close?" is the format candidates say they answer, [T-candidate-b-22]) and carry it as structured data all the way into ShiftGrid, so the rota can see it.

> **Insight:** The hired-to-rota gap is the most expensive window in the process and nobody owns it. Between the verbal yes and the first schedulable shift, the candidate is a promise in every system and an employee in none.
> **Evidence:** `discovery/systems-and-data.md` key finding (no system owns the hired to on-rota transition); [T-yolanda-20] (Nómina360 creation takes 3 to 6 days and gates scheduling, "full stop"); [T-sofia-18] (payroll calls the person "a promise"; managers want them on Saturday); [T-hugo-12] (ShiftGrid locks the rota only on Thursdays); [T-yolanda-10] (11 days observed for her last hire); funnel output in `discovery/funnel-baseline.md` (on-rota step: 5.1 median days, the largest time sink; day-1 no-show 17.9%).
> **So what for design:** the agent should own this window end to end with a visible per-person status: pre-collect the three documents that are always late ([T-sofia-10]), track the Nómina360 creation, and confirm the first shift with the new hire directly, so a yes stops being a hope.

> **Insight:** The managers already run the informal talent pool the system lacks, on personal WhatsApp, and the official stack both ignores it and depends on it. Any tool that competes with the bench loses; one that adopts it wins.
> **Evidence:** [T-yolanda-14, T-yolanda-16] (about 25 "maybes" in her personal WhatsApp; hired before any requisition exists); [T-yolanda-08] (her last hire was contacted by her first, inverting the funnel); [T-yolanda-18] and [T-jordi-18, T-jordi-20] (bench hires logged post-hoc, which is why the Head of TA calls his own funnel reports garbage); [T-hugo-26] and [T-laura-12] (that channel is shadow IT and a GDPR blind spot the DPO cannot even search).
> **So what for design:** make the bench a first-class, manager-owned object with consent and logging, reachable from the same WhatsApp the managers already use, rather than another queue in HireDesk. The ride-along shows none of a Saturday's hiring happened in HireDesk (`discovery/ride-along-benidorm.md`, Counts section).

> **Insight:** Week-1 attrition is expectation and rota mismatch, not job difficulty. The leavers liked the work; what broke was the schedule they never agreed to and the silence when they asked.
> **Evidence:** [T-iker-14 to T-iker-16] (2 of his last 5 quit in week 1, both with the same words: this is not what I was told); [T-churned-08, T-churned-18, T-churned-24] (first rota the night before at 22:30, a shift against her declared evening-only timetable, a swap request left on read, and "the work I liked"); [T-churned-26] (exit recorded as "personal reasons"; the real cause is operational); [T-candidate-a-16, T-candidate-a-18] (first shift learned by chance from a crew WhatsApp group); funnel output in `discovery/funnel-baseline.md` (91 of 325 day-1 workers, 28.0%, gone by day 30).
> **So what for design:** a day-3 WhatsApp check-in that reads back the promised schedule against the real one, plus an answerable channel for swap requests, is the cheapest retention lever found. Exit data should capture the operational cause, not the embarrassment.

> **Insight:** The screening criteria filter out exactly the profile that converts best for weekend shifts, and the Head of TA knows it and cannot say it in meetings. CV completeness is a proxy for nothing the rota needs.
> **Evidence:** [T-jordi-06, T-jordi-08] ("complete CV first" in practice; students and first-jobbers "fall out of the pile without anyone having read them"); [T-jordi-10] (the perfect weekend-shift candidate saved by luck, via a WhatsApp group, not by the tool); [T-candidate-a-06, T-candidate-a-20] (first-job applications are thin by nature and the form gets answered untruthfully); funnel output in `discovery/funnel-baseline.md` (only 1,991 of 4,500 applications, 44.2%, reached screened); [T-jordi-14] (the pile is reviewed "if there is air", which in season means almost never).
> **So what for design:** rank by shift fit and concrete availability, never by CV completeness; present a thin-CV candidate with one question instead of a verdict; the recommendation is the agent's, the rejection stays human ([T-jordi-22, T-jordi-24]).

> **Insight:** In a tourist drive-thru market, language skill is the most valuable field nobody collects. Two years of applications, zero language questions, in a town where half the drive-thru cars are foreign.
> **Evidence:** [T-candidate-c-08, T-candidate-c-10] (speaks Portuguese, Spanish and English; nobody in two years and two drive-thru applications asked one question about it); [T-candidate-c-24] ("it is the most important thing here, more than the experience"); [T-iker-18] (customer-facing ability "is gold here with the tourists"); `specs/00-client-brief.md` section 4 (coastal drive-thru units, 7.5% of peak hours understaffed in July and August, service time 312 s understaffed vs 228 s fully staffed).
> **So what for design:** one plain language question in the first chat, stored as a tagged fact on the profile and matched to unit needs during screening. It is a conversation fact, not a scored test, and it turns "seasonal" CVs from a rejection signal into an availability-now signal ([T-candidate-c-18]).
